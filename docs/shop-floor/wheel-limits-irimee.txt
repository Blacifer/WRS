WHEEL SET LIMITS FOR C&W STOCK — FROM INDIAN RAILWAYS' OWN TRAINING NOTES
Source: IRIMEE (Indian Railways Institute of Mechanical and Electrical Engineering) supervisors'
training centre notes, "Wheel Set / Wheel Defects" (STC/NBQ) and "Types of Bogies in Wagon
Stock" (rskr.irimee.in), read September 2026. Source label in this index: WHEEL_LIMITS.
The workshop should confirm these against IRCA Part III / the Wagon Maintenance Manual before
relying on them for a release; they are reproduced here so that every wheel reading can cite
the figure it was judged against.

WHEEL GAUGE
The wheel gauge distance for BG (C&W) wheel is 1600 +2/-1 mm.

WHEEL PROFILE
Flange thickness: 29.4 mm (worn wheel profile); condemning 16 mm, and 22 mm for high speed.
Flange thickness is reckoned at a distance of approximately 13 mm from the flange tip.
Flange height: new 28.5 mm.
Radius at the root of flange: new 14 mm, condemning 13 mm.
Radius at top of flange: 14.5 mm.
Inclination of 1 in 2.5 on flange; 1 in 20 on tread.

WHEEL DEFECTS — STANDARD AND CONDEMNING LIMIT
Sharp flange — standard 14.5 mm — condemn at 5 mm or less.
Thin flange — standard 29.4 mm — condemn at 16 mm or less (22 mm high speed).
Less radius at root of flange — standard 14 mm — condemn at 13 mm or less.
Hollow tyre — condemn at 5 mm or above.
Deep flange — standard 28.5 mm — condemn at 35 mm or more (measured from the flange tip to a
point on the wheel tread 63.5 mm from the back of the wheel).
Flat tyre — condemn at 50 mm or more for coaching stock, 60 mm or more for goods stock (all BG
wagons); locomotives 50 mm.

OTHER REJECTABLE CONDITIONS (per CMI-K003)
Shattered rim: a wheel with a fracture on the tread or flange must be withdrawn from service.
Spread rim: rim widening for a short distance on the front face — an internal defect may be
present; such wheels must be withdrawn.
Thermal cracks: from severe brake binding; when a crack becomes visible on the outer face of
the rim, reaches the outer edge of the rim, or is visible on the outer flange face, the wheel
must be withdrawn and sent to workshop.
Heat checks: fine superficial cracks on or near the braking surface; need not be withdrawn but
must be distinguished from rejectable thermal cracks.
Built-up tread: metal from tread or brake shoe dragged around the tread, associated with
sliding on the rail.
Wheel shelling: rolling contact fatigue; pieces of metal breaking out of the tread surface.
